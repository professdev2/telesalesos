Handling the "I need to talk to my daughter" objection

Chapter 2 · Holding the emotional frame

TranscriptChapters 4Notes 2Resources 3
3:12So she says, "I need to talk to my daughter first." Most agents hear a no. It isn't. It's a permission problem — she wants someone she trusts to tell her this is okay.
3:31"Mrs. Whitcombe, that makes total sense — is your daughter the one who'd be handling things if something happened?" Notice I'm not asking her to skip the daughter. I'm putting the daughter in the room.
3:58Two beats. Then: "Would it help if we got her on the line right now, so she hears it from me and not secondhand?" Half the time she says yes. The other half, you've just learned the real objection is the payment, and that's a different conversation — lesson 9.
4:20What you never do here is quote a number. The moment you quote before the frame is set, you've handed her a reason to hang up and "think about it" …
